# Research Results and Limitations

## Retained methodology

- Static behavioral segmentation separates retail and enterprise populations using reproducible feature preparation and cluster evaluation.
- Forecasting compares static segment pools with temporal customer-outlook pools and records model selection separately from deployment integration.
- Product propensity uses an explicit feature list, customer-level holdouts, probability outputs, and contract validation.
- Hybrid recommendations combine propensity candidates with ontology needs, eligibility, prerequisites, conflicts, alternatives, ownership exclusion, scoring, and provenance.
- RAG uses public evidence retrieval with lexical/dense/hybrid retrieval boundaries and citation-aware answer generation.
- LangGraph routes public questions, authenticated customer context, recommendation explanations, and unsupported actions separately.

## Reduced-catalogue demonstration boundary

The portfolio demonstration retains nine approved catalogue-level product examples, filtered ownership and recommendation fixtures, and a small grounded-resource set. These resources exist only to exercise the architecture and ontology rules. They are not a replacement for the original research population.

Original research metrics and full-scale evaluation outputs are kept separate from the reduced-catalogue demonstration results and are not presented as comparable measurements.

## Limitations

- Customer, ownership, recommendation, and retrieval fixtures are synthetic. Product names and basic catalogue labels are retained only at the approved public-information level.
- No trained binary model or vector index is included. Model training and index construction are reproducible steps, not committed outputs.
- The original fitted segmentation estimators were not portable artifacts, so this edition retains the implementation and evaluation methodology but does not claim estimator restoration.
- Synthetic propensity labels are not observed adoption or conversion outcomes.
- Retrieval metrics are not proof of factual completeness; human review remains required for claims and publication clearance.
